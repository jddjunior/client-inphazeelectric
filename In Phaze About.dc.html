THIRTY YEARS · SIXTEEN IN BUSINESS

Navy discipline. Orlando addresses.

Tony Taylor learned to do electrical work where "close enough" got people hurt. Sixteen years later that's still the operating standard — which is the only reason {{ reviewCount }} strangers have bothered to write about an electrician.

ON SITE · WINTER PARK

The whole business is one habit.

Tell people the truth about their electrical system, even when the truth costs you the invoice. It sounds like a slogan until you read the reviews and find the same story over and over: someone came out to quote a panel replacement and left saying the panel was fine.

That habit is why this shop grew without a sales department. Customers come back for the second job, the third job, the rental property, and then they send their neighbor. In sixteen years we've never had to advertise our way out of a bad reputation — just into a bigger service area.

Today it's a fleet of purple trucks across Orange, Seminole and Osceola counties, a licensed crew you can ask for by name, and an office that files your permit so you never have to call the county.

"If you don't need it, we'll tell you. That's the entire business plan."
