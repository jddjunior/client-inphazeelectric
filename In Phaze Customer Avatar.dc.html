01 — The primary avatar

What makes Denise legible in the data

Household

Married or widowed homeowner. Adult or teenage kids. Often two decision-makers, but she books the appointment and writes the review.

Geography

Inside the 25-mile Orlando ring: Winter Park, Maitland, Altamonte, Longwood, Lake Mary, Sanford, Apopka, Ocoee, Casselberry, Kissimmee and Deltona.

The house

Owned 8–25 years. Florida block construction, 1950s–1990s. Original or once-upgraded panel, vaulted ceilings, a lanai, a detached garage and at least one circuit nobody can explain.

Buying state

Second or third call, not first. She has already been ghosted, over-quoted or told she needs a full rewire.

Work and technology

Frequently works from home. Wants texts, an ETA and a tracker link. Notices technicians by name and will not tolerate a phone tree.

Risk she feels

Two fears stacked together: fire in her house, and being taken by a man in her house who knows more than she does.

She buys a verdict she can trust, delivered by a person whose name she will remember. Over half of substantive reviews credit a specific technician. She reports the price as fair rather than cheap and describes manners, shoes, cleanup and tone with the same weight she gives the wiring.

03 — The decision sequence

How she actually gets here

→ 1

Something breaks, or a deadline appears

She waits days to weeks because electrical work combines fear of cost with fear of the wiring.

→ 2

She calls someone else first

She is ghosted, quoted double or upsold into a rewire she does not need.

→ 3

She reads reviews as a background check

Google first, then Angi or HomeAdvisor, then neighbor or realtor referrals. She scans for showing up and holding the price.

→ 4

The estimate is the purchase decision

Free, in-person and no-pressure is what earns the hire. One reviewer calls In Phaze the only contractor offering it.

→ 5

The visit creates a repeat client

About one in seven substantive reviews mentions a prior job; one customer counts seven. There is little middle ground.

04 — The decision rules

What earns the yes, what loses her

Earns the yes

  • A named human with an ETA, call, text and tracker link.
  • A free, in-person, no-pressure estimate.
  • The quote holding exactly.
  • Plain-language explanations.
  • Being told she needs less than she feared.
  • Neatness: shoes off, drop cloths, vacuuming and labeled breakers.
  • Permits handled for her.
  • Honest speed, not manufactured urgency.

Loses her — usually forever

  • A no-call, no-show.
  • Scare tactics and full-rewire upsells.
  • Commission-flavored upselling.
  • Leaving drywall open or the job unfinished.
  • Price that outruns visible work.
  • Silence after the deposit.

07 — Her own words

The voice bank

Use these phrasings; do not invent new ones.

  • “No added extras or scare tactics” — make no scare tactics an explicit promise.
  • “Free HONEST estimate” — pair free estimate with truth.
  • “Stuck to the estimate precisely” — offer a quote-lock guarantee.
  • “Explained it in simple terms” — market the explanation, not the diagnosis.
  • “Took his shoes off at the door” — codify home respect.
  • “Made sure my mind was at ease” — sell relief, not amperage.
  • “Someone I can trust” — offer a named-tech request path.
08 — Applying it

Message to the avatar, not the category

Say this

  • “We'll tell you if you don't need it.”
  • “The quote you get is the price you pay.”
  • “You'll know who's coming, and when.”
  • “Free, in person, no pressure.”
  • “Already tried someone else?”
  • “We pull the permit and meet the inspector.”
  • “Hablamos español.”
  • “We patch the wall before we leave.”

Never say this

  • Fear-first headlines about danger or fire.
  • Teaser rates such as “Starting at $99.”
  • Countdown timers or manufactured urgency.
  • Amperage, code sections or panel models in the headline.
  • Stock photos of anonymous electricians.
  • Emergency promises unless dispatch is genuinely bulletproof.
09 — Method

How this was built, and what it cannot tell you

Public review data was collected from six platforms for In Phaze Electric, Inc. Roughly 95 reviews were read verbatim and coded for trigger, job type, disclosed price, emotional language, named staff, competitor references, repeat-purchase signals and demographic markers. Aggregate counts and star distributions come from the platforms’ summaries.

Review populations self-select toward extremes and customers comfortable writing online. Ages, incomes and household details are inferred rather than stated. Yelp’s listings could not be read directly. Testimonials on the company website were used only to corroborate themes already visible in third-party reviews. Median price comes from 35 disclosed job costs, a small and probably non-random sample.

“Denise” is a composite, not an individual reviewer. Every quoted line is a real public review attributed to its platform and date. Prepared 21 August 2026.
