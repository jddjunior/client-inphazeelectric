WHY PEOPLE STAY

Sixteen years, and the crew is still the crew.

This trade churns people. Ours mostly do not leave, and the reason is boring: predictable work, decent trucks, and a boss who has done the job for thirty years and does not pretend otherwise.

Take-home truck & tools

Stocked, maintained, and yours. You are not buying your own material or fuelling your own van.

No 60-hour weeks as policy

Storm season happens and we all show up. It is not the baseline expectation the rest of the year.

Paid continuing education

CEUs, license renewals and exam fees covered. If you want your own license eventually, we will help you get there.

Work you can sign

We do not bid jobs we have to cut corners to win. You will never be told to skip the permit or leave a wall open.

Your name on the page

Real profile, real review count, customers requesting you by name. That is portable reputation, not just a job.

Veteran friendly

Tony is Navy. If you came out of the service with electrical experience, you will not have to explain your résumé here.

The office actually works

Permits filed, inspections booked, customers pre-briefed. You are not doing dispatch from your truck.

Growing on purpose

We are scaling deliberately, which means new trucks and lead roles opening rather than headcount churn.

HOW HIRING GOES

Four steps. About a week.

No panel interviews, no personality quizzes, no six-week silence. You will talk to the owner, not a recruiter.

STEP 01
You apply below

Name, phone, what you have worked on. A résumé is welcome but not required — photos of your work say more.

STEP 02
Tony calls you

Usually within two business days. Fifteen minutes, straight questions, and he will tell you the pay range on that call.

STEP 03
Ride along a day

Paid. You see how we work, we see how you work, and both of us find out more in eight hours than in three interviews.

STEP 04
Offer, in writing

Background and driving check, then a written offer with the number, the truck, and the start date on it.
